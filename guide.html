What matters most

Lab-grown or natural

They are the same stone. The only difference is where it came from.

A lab-grown diamond is a diamond — same carbon, same hardness, same sparkle. It is not a simulant; cubic zirconia and moissanite are the lookalikes, and those are different materials entirely. Only a laboratory can tell a lab-grown stone from a mined one.

The real difference is cost, and what happens afterwards.

Lab-grown

Grown in weeks
  • Much cheaper — roughly 10–20% of a natural stone at the same colour and clarity, so about 80–90% less.
  • More stone for the money — you get a visibly larger, cleaner diamond for the same spend.
  • Keeps getting cheaper — prices have fallen sharply year on year as production scales up.
  • Resale is effectively nil. This is the honest catch.
  • Graded by IGI or GIA, and often graded a little more generously.

Natural

Formed over billions of years
  • Substantially more expensive — the same specs can run five to ten times the price.
  • Holds some value — a natural stone typically resells for roughly 20–40% of retail.
  • Stable pricing — it has not collapsed the way lab-grown has.
  • Rarity is the whole point for some people; for others it simply is not.
  • Usually GIA-graded, which is the stricter of the two labs.
In plain terms: lab-grown buys a bigger, better-looking stone today; natural holds more of its value later. Neither is the wrong answer — it is worth deciding which of those two things you actually care about.

The four things that decide a diamond

Everyone calls them the four Cs. They are not equally important.

1. Cut — by far the most important

How well the angles inside the stone throw light back at you. A beautifully cut smaller diamond looks better than a badly cut bigger one, every time. It is also the only thing on this list that comes down to the cutter's skill rather than the raw stone — which is why it is the one worth being fussy about.

Round diamonds get an official cut grade. Other shapes do not — for those you judge it by eye, which is exactly what trying them on is for.

2. Colour

Runs from D (completely colourless) down to Z (visibly yellow). Most people cannot tell a G from a D once the stone is set and on a hand — and in yellow or rose gold you can go further down the scale without it showing at all.

D–FG–HI–JK–MN–Z

3. Clarity

How many internal marks the stone has, from Flawless down to I3 (visible to the naked eye). The sweet spot sits around VS2–SI1: clean to the eye, and far less expensive than the near-perfect grades almost nobody can see the difference from.

FLVVSVSSII

4. Carat

The weight, not the size. Two stones of the same weight can look different sizes depending on how deeply they are cut. Prices also jump at round numbers, so a 0.95ct stone can cost noticeably less than a 1.00ct one and look identical on a hand.

Two more worth knowing

Fluorescence — a faint glow under UV light. Usually harmless, but strong fluorescence can make a stone look slightly milky in daylight.

The report — every stone should come with a GIA or IGI certificate, and the number on it can be checked online. Worth asking for, every time.
